# Design: Greyline receiver map

Generated by /office-hours on 2026-10-20
Branch: main
Repo: thehashrocket/skywave
Status: APPROVED
Mode: Builder

## Problem Statement

skywave picks receivers from a table (`ReceiverPicker.tsx`) ranked by load and SNR. The public KiwiSDR list knows where each of ~850 receivers is, but none of that shows. Where a receiver is matters most on shortwave: a receiver on the night side of the Earth, or along the day/night line, hears distant broadcasters that a daylit one can't. README "Next steps" 1 asks for a map that lets you pick receivers by clicking.

## What Makes This Cool

Existing KiwiSDR maps (map.kiwisdr.com, rx.kiwisdr.com, ve3sun, Priyom) are directories: dots, a pop-up, then you leave the map to listen. skywave's map sits next to a live receiver and a known frequency, so it can act as a live propagation display:
- The greyline (day/night terminator) moves across the map in real time.
- Dots show which receivers are in the dark right now, which are full, and which one you're hearing; receivers that can't tune the dial's frequency dim.
- Clicking a dot in the dark half tunes it.

## Constraints

- Personal tool / side project (builder mode). No accounts or API keys.
- The map must not slow the radio down: audio and the waterfall start first.
- ~730 listed receivers, about half in Europe and up to 7 at one site, so the map must zoom, stay smooth, and handle overlapping dots.
- The public list is fetched server-side every 15 minutes (`receivers.ts`) and lives in memory.
- Works on a phone as well as a desktop.

## Premises

Agreed in session:
1. The map is an always-on panel on the main page: the greyline now, the receivers that cover the dial's frequency, and the one you're on. Clicking a dot tunes it, the same as choosing it in the table. The table and Pick best stay.
2. Coordinates come from the list we already fetch (its `gps` field, e.g. `"(59.546000, 12.526000)"`). Receivers with no usable GPS stay in the table but aren't drawn. No new data source.
3. The greyline is computed in the browser from the sun's position and redrawn every minute. No plugin.
4. No `Receiver` database table now. host:port is already a receiver's identity everywhere (`receiverKey`), so Preset's existing `receiverHost`/`receiverPort` columns are the right shape; wire them up when presets get a "pin to this receiver" button. The TODOS entry that expected a foreign key should say this instead.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Leaflet + raster tiles
Rejected: ~850 DOM markers lag in crowded Europe, restyling by frequency is manual, and the free raster tile sources have usage limits.

### Approach C: Own world canvas (Natural Earth coastlines, Geochron-style)
Rejected for now: we'd build pan, zoom and hit-testing ourselves for crowded regions. Kept as a style idea (see Open Questions).

## Recommended Approach

**Approach B: MapLibre GL JS with OpenFreeMap vector tiles**, chosen because the always-on map keeps ~730 dots (362 of them in Europe) smooth to pan and zoom, and restyles them every minute as the night moves. MapLibre does that as one GPU layer with per-feature state, and OpenFreeMap needs no key.

Measured on the 2026-10-20 list (after the `ext_api=0` filter): 730 receivers, all with usable GPS; 586 (80%) cover 0-30 MHz, the rest start at 10 kHz to 1.8 MHz or reach 32 MHz; 44 were full; up to 7 receivers share one exact coordinate (multi-port sites).

**Data**
- `kiwi-list.ts`: parse `gps` into `lat`/`lon` on `Receiver` (both null when missing, unparseable, out of range, or `(0, 0)`). Keep the rest of `toReceiver` unchanged; receivers without coordinates still appear in the table.
- Frequency coverage is the existing `bands` field, already parsed into `minKhz`/`maxKhz` and tested by `covers(r, freqKhz)`. Because most receivers cover all of HF, coverage is a secondary cue (dim when the dial is outside a receiver's range, which mostly matters on LW/MW and above 30 MHz), not the main visual.
- The map draws the same filtered list the table renders (`ext_api=0` receivers stay out). No new endpoint.

**What the dots show** (in priority order)
1. Playing: amber, pulsing, labeled.
2. Night at the receiver: brighter, on a darker night side. This is daylight context and a hint, not a ranking: night often helps HF reception but doesn't prove a receiver hears the station on the dial (that needs the transmitter's location). Pick best stays the recommender, and the UI copy says so. (R9)
3. Full: hollow ring. The shared `receivers.list` query refetches every 15 minutes while the tab is visible (R8), so load state is at most ~30 minutes old (server cache plus interval); the map shows the list's age when it's stale, like the table.
4. Out of range for the dial: dimmed.

**Modules**
- `apps/web/src/lib/solar.ts`: sun position, elevation at a point, night and twilight regions.
- `apps/web/src/lib/receiver-map-model.ts` (R2): pure functions for features from the filtered list, hit grouping (≤10 per card), the follow state (follow / user-moved / re-center) and feature-state diffs; unit-tested.
- `apps/web/src/components/MapPanel.tsx`: always rendered (no MapLibre import): heading, legend, pre-load shell, state overlays; lazy-loads the adapter once admitted and never unmounts it on receiver switches.
- `apps/web/src/components/ReceiverMap.tsx`: thin MapLibre adapter that feeds events to the model; tested with `maplibre-gl` mocked.
- `apps/web/src/lib/receiver-map-style.ts`: pure `buildStyle(tokens)` and dot paint expressions, with token fallbacks.
- `apps/web/src/lib/use-solar-clock.ts`: page-level minute clock read by both the table's Night indicator and the map.

**Greyline and per-receiver darkness**
- One solar function gives the sun's declination and the subsolar longitude for a time. From it: the terminator as the line where the sun's elevation is -0.833° (sunrise/sunset, with refraction and the solar radius), and each receiver's elevation.
- Night region (R6): the set of points where the sun is below -0.833° is a spherical cap of angular radius 90° - 0.833° = 89.167° centered on the anti-solar point (latitude -declination, longitude subsolar + 180°). Build its boundary as a small circle (1° azimuth steps), then: if the cap contains a pole (|declination| > 0.833°), close the ring along that pole's clamped edge (±85.05°); otherwise it is a closed loop, split where it crosses ±180°. Clamp latitudes to ±85.05° for Web Mercator.
- Civil-twilight band: the same construction with radius 84° (sun below -6°), drawn lighter; it contains a pole only when |declination| > 6°, so for weeks around each equinox it's a closed loop too.
- Every 60 s, recompute both regions (`setData` on the greyline source) and each receiver's `night` flag (`setFeatureState`, no source rebuild). The receivers source uses `promoteId` on the receiver key so ids are stable, and night and outOfRange state are re-applied right after every list update (R3).
- Dial changes update only the `outOfRange` state, coalesced to one update per animation frame.

**Interaction**
- Every device: a click or tap selects and opens the card; only its **Listen** button switches receivers (design review D6, superseding the eng review's desktop click-to-tune). Listen goes through the page's `chooseReceiver`, the table's own path, which cancels a running Pick best before `tuneReceiver(r, true)` (R1). On desktop, hover shows a read-only tooltip. Choosing a full receiver behaves exactly as choosing it in the table.
- The card is a React overlay positioned with `map.project`, not a MapLibre Popup, so Listen is a real `<button>` and the click is a user gesture that can start audio (R5).
- Overlapping dots: when a click or tap hits more than one receiver (same site, or dots closer than the hit radius), the card lists them all (up to 10, then "zoom in for more"), each with its own Listen button. No clustering, so every receiver keeps its own dot and night state.
- View: the map starts on longitude -180..180, latitude -58..75. It follows the playing receiver until the user pans or zooms; after that it stays put until **Center on receiver** is pressed (design review D8, superseding the eng review's "resume on next tune").

**Loading order**
- `ReceiverMap` mounts only after the first audio frame of a session arrives (`listening` is sent before audio, `session.py:255`), or after 5 s with nothing playing and no connection in progress; then on `requestIdleCallback`, falling back to `setTimeout(…, 200)` where it's missing (Safari) (R7, R4). `use-radio` exposes a per-session "first audio received" flag for this.
- The map shows the OpenStreetMap/OpenMapTiles attribution that OpenFreeMap tiles require (MapLibre's attribution control, compact).
- No WebGL, a style that fails to load, or 5 tile errors within 30 s: the panel shows a short note ("Map unavailable: <reason>") and stops trying. The table and Pick best keep working.

**Layout**
- Desktop: the map is a panel on the main page beside or above the waterfall. Phone: a shorter strip under the dial. A visual pass (`/plan-design-review`) settles the exact layout; mockup generation failed in this session (API rate limits).

**Out of scope here:** transmitter paths (needs station lookup, README next step 2), "hear it from there" click-anywhere tuning, presets pinned to receivers, a `Receiver` table.

## Success Criteria

- No map request (library, style or tile) starts before the session's first audio frame, or before 5 s idle with no connection in progress; browsers without `requestIdleCallback` still mount it.
- All receivers with valid GPS appear; turning the dial updates out-of-range dots within one animation frame, with no visible stutter.
- The night and twilight regions match NOAA-computed sun elevations at sample points for a June solstice, a December solstice, an equinox and ±0.5°/±6° declinations (inside the region iff elevation < threshold).
- On every device, clicking or tapping a dot opens its card without changing what's playing; Listen tunes it, the dot turns amber once listening, and the table's "playing" mark agrees.
- A click on a multi-receiver site lists every receiver there.
- With WebGL disabled, or the style failing to load, the page still works and says why the map is missing.
- Tests: GPS parsing (valid, missing, malformed, out of range, `(0, 0)`); the night cap contains a pole only when |declination| > 0.833° (twilight: 6°), is a closed loop split at ±180° otherwise, and stays within ±85.05°; a receiver inside the night polygon is flagged night and one in daylight isn't; overlapping hits produce a list; follow stops after a user pan and resumes only on Center; the map doesn't mount between `listening` and the first audio frame; a map pick cancels a running Pick best; night/outOfRange survive a list refetch.

Ships with the existing web app deployment; no new artifact. One new dependency (`maplibre-gl`), lazy-loaded. Tiles come from OpenFreeMap at runtime.

### R1-1 — completeness (blocking)

**Problem**

> The core styling rule 'covers the dial's frequency' is never defined: the design does not say which field of the KiwiSDR list (e.g. a bands/frequency-range field) supplies coverage, whether Receiver already carries it, or how it is parsed. Most KiwiSDRs cover 0-30 MHz, so unless coverage data exists and varies, the bright/dim restyle on every dial move (the stated reason for choosing MapLibre) may change almost nothing. The recommendation depends on this unverified claim.

**Remedy**

> Name the list field that defines frequency coverage, add its parsing to the Data section and tests, and confirm it varies enough across receivers to justify the per-dial-move restyle; if it does not, revise the dot semantics and the rationale for Approach B.

### R1-2 — feasibility (blocking)

**Problem**

> Overlapping dots are not handled. About 425 receivers sit in Europe, several share a host or city, and at world zoom many dots overlap. 'Click a dot tunes it' has no rule for a click that hits several features. Approach C was rejected precisely because of hit-testing in crowded regions, but Approach B does not specify clustering, spiderfying, a chooser, or zoom-to-expand either.

**Remedy**

> Choose and document the behavior when a click or tap hits more than one receiver (e.g. a small chooser list, zoom-in, or clustering with expansion), and add it to the tests.

### R1-3 — consistency (blocking)

**Problem**

> On a phone, 'Click a dot: tune' and 'Hover or tap shows name, location, users/max and SNR' assign the same gesture to two different actions. As written, a tap meant to inspect a receiver would also switch the live receiver, and the constraint says the map must work on a phone.

**Remedy**

> Define the touch interaction explicitly, e.g. first tap shows the popup with a Tune button and a second action tunes, or tap tunes and long-press inspects.

### R1-4 — feasibility (blocking)

**Problem**

> The night-side polygon in Web Mercator is underspecified: the night region contains a pole for most of the year, Mercator cannot render beyond about +/-85 degrees, and the polygon must be split or wrapped at the antimeridian. A naive polygon renders inverted or with seams. The 'soft band' along the terminator (width, twilight definition) is also unspecified.

**Remedy**

> Specify how the night polygon is constructed (which pole it closes to based on declination sign, clamping to the Mercator latitude limit, antimeridian handling or world-copy behavior) and what the soft band represents (e.g. civil twilight to -6 degrees), and test the equinox case where declination is near zero.

### R1-5 — completeness (blocking)

**Problem**

> Dots are styled 'in darkness (slightly brighter)', but the design does not say how each receiver's day/night state is computed or refreshed. It needs a per-feature sun-elevation calculation, re-run every 60 s along with the greyline, and the GeoJSON source is only described as rebuilt when the list changes.

**Remedy**

> State that the per-receiver darkness flag comes from the same solar function as the greyline, when it is recomputed (every 60 s alongside the polygon), and how the layer is updated (source setData or feature-state), with a test that a point inside the polygon is flagged dark.

### R1-6 — feasibility (blocking)

**Problem**

> The constraint 'audio and the waterfall start first' and the success criterion 'the radio plays before the map finishes loading' are not guaranteed by next/dynamic alone. An always-on panel mounts immediately and then downloads MapLibre, its worker, the style, fonts and tiles in parallel with the radio stream, which can delay audio start on a phone connection.

**Remedy**

> Specify when the map mounts relative to the radio (e.g. after audio starts or on idle via requestIdleCallback) so the ordering is a defined behavior, not a side effect of code-splitting.

### R1-7 — clarity (blocking)

**Problem**

> 'The view follows the receiver you're on unless you've panned' does not say when following resumes (never, on the next tune, after a timeout, or via a re-center control), or what the initial view is before any receiver plays.

**Remedy**

> Define the follow and resume rules and the initial map view (e.g. world view at start; tuning re-enables follow; a re-center button).

### R1-8 — completeness (minor)

**Problem**

> The design does not say that the map draws the same filtered list as the table. Recent work excludes receivers that refuse apps from the list, and the map should not reintroduce them. Behavior when a full receiver is clicked is likewise implied only by 'same path as onChoose'.

**Remedy**

> State that the map's GeoJSON is built from the same filtered list the table renders, and that clicking a full (hollow) receiver behaves exactly as choosing it in the table.

### R1-9 — clarity (minor)

**Problem**

> The greyline accuracy criterion ('within about one degree' of timeanddate.com) does not say whether the terminator is geometric (sun center at 0 degrees) or includes refraction and the solar radius (-0.833 degrees). That choice alone shifts the line by roughly a degree.

**Remedy**

> Pick the terminator definition and test against computed reference values rather than a visual comparison.

### R1-10 — completeness (minor)

**Problem**

> OpenFreeMap/OpenMapTiles tiles require OpenStreetMap attribution, which the design does not mention. Tile-failure detection is also vague: the design does not say how mid-session or partial tile failures are recognized, as opposed to a style that never loads.

**Remedy**

> Note that the map shows the required attribution, and define the tile-failure trigger (e.g. style load error or repeated tile errors) for the fallback note.

### R1-11 — clarity (minor)

**Problem**

> The full/hollow and users/max states come from a list refreshed every 15 minutes, so they can be stale. The design does not say whether that staleness is acceptable or how rapid dial movement is throttled before restyling.

**Remedy**

> Acknowledge the 15-minute staleness of load state on the map (as in the table), and say whether dial-driven restyles are throttled, e.g. to animation frames.

### R1-12 — clarity (minor)

**Problem**

> Open Question 2 (whether the app sets a CSP) is a fact that can be checked in the repo, not a discovery question, and it affects whether MapLibre's worker and tile fetches work at all.

**Remedy**

> Check the Next.js config and headers for a CSP and record the answer and any required connect-src/worker-src/img-src entries in the design.
